Introduction

What is Mountaineering?

Mountaineering, also known as alpinism, is the sport, hobby or profession of ascending mountains.

It grew from a simple desire to reach a summit into a sophisticated discipline combining physical endurance, technical skill and mental resilience. Mountaineers navigate rock, snow and ice, often at extreme altitudes where every decision carries life-or-death weight.

Beyond the physical challenge, mountaineering teaches teamwork, planning, humility and respect for nature. It is as much a journey inward as it is an ascent upward.

Timeline

History of Mountaineering

From the golden age of alpinism to modern high-altitude expeditions.

1786

The Birth of Alpinism

Jacques Balmat and Michel-Gabriel Paccard make the first ascent of Mont Blanc, marking the birth of mountaineering as a sport.

1865

The Golden Age

Edward Whymper's party summits the Matterhorn, capping the "golden age" of Alpine first ascents.

1950

The First Eight-Thousander

Annapurna I is climbed by Herzog and Lachenal — the first 8,000m peak ever summited.

1953

Everest Conquered

Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay reach the summit of Mount Everest, the world's highest mountain.

1978

The Oxygen-Free Era

Messner and Habeler climb Everest without supplemental oxygen, redefining the limits of human endurance.

1970s–90s

The Winter & Alpine Style Era

Climbers pioneer winter ascents and lightweight alpine-style climbing on the highest peaks.

2019

Project Possible

Nirmal Purja climbs all 14 eight-thousanders in just 6 months and 6 days, shattering every record.
